Diagnosis

What this notice means

For a given trip_id, the arrival_time of (n+1)-th stoptime in sequence must not precede the departure_time of n-th stoptime in sequence in stop_times.txt.

Operational effect

Why it matters

The vehicle arrives at a stop before it left the one before it, so the trip travels backwards in time. Journey planners either reject the trip or show a nonsensical itinerary for it.

Scope

Affected files and report fields

  • stop_times.txtOpen GTFS specification
Report fieldTypeMeaning

departureTimeobjectDeparture time at the previous stop time.

tripIdstringThe trip_id associated to the faulty record.

Diagnosis

What usually causes it

  • A trip that runs past midnight with later stops written as 00:10:00 instead of 24:10:00.
  • Stop times interpolated or rounded by a scheduling tool until two adjacent stops crossed over.
  • Rows reordered by stop_id or by time rather than by stop_sequence during an export.
  • A layover or a timing point edited in isolation without shifting the rest of the trip.

Check both sides. Read the whole trip in stop_sequence order, not just the flagged row — one mistyped time makes the row after it look wrong too.

Repair

How to fix it

Correct the times so that each stop's arrival is at or after the previous stop's departure along the whole `stop_sequence`. Where the trip crosses midnight, keep counting past 24 rather than wrapping to 00.

  1. 1
    Locate the record

    Open stop_times.txt and use the report fields below to find the exact row.

  2. 2
    Change the source data

    Apply the repair described above to that record, and leave unrelated rows alone.

  3. 3
    Validate again

    Rebuild the GTFS archive and confirm that stop_time_with_arrival_before_previous_departure_time no longer appears for the record.

Do not do this: Do not nudge the later stop forward by a minute just to clear the check. If the real cause is a midnight wrap, the trip is still a day out and the published times will not match what the vehicle does.
